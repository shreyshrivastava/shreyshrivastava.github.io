How I build

  1. Rules first, model second

    In the resume checker, the healthcare demo and the honeypot, the score or decision comes from plain rules. A model only explains or assists, so the answer is the same every run.

  2. Limits next to results

    Each project says what it does not do. The Bhopal map shows no risk scores, the arbitrage bot places no trades, and the claim checker is not a fact-checker.

  3. Small samples called small

    agent-ops ran 8 tasks twice each and says so. The numbers are a rough guide, not a claim.

Learned the hard way

  1. A review caught what I missed

    Before I shared agent-ops, a review found two bugs: truncated files could be written back with content missing, and command arguments could reach outside the workspace. I fixed both, with tests.

  2. A model can fail the same way every time

    On my benchmark, no run got Indian digit grouping (₹1,23,456.78) right. Every failure was the model looping until it hit the step limit.

  3. The big model does not fit everywhere

    My dissertation model is about 500 MB, too big for a serverless demo. The app falls back through four modes, down to plain rules, so it never crashes.
